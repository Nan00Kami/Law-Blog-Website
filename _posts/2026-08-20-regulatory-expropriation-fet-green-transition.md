The entry into full fiscal effect of border carbon adjustment mechanisms—most prominently the European Union’s Carbon Border Adjustment Mechanism (CBAM)—marks an unprecedented integration of trade policy with climate regulation. 

By requiring importers to purchase emissions certificates corresponding to the carbon embedded in imported goods, CBAM aims to prevent "carbon leakage." However, its design poses acute legal challenges under the multilateral framework of the General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade (GATT 1994).

---

## 1. Prima Facie Inconsistencies: GATT Articles I, III, and II

A legal challenge against a border carbon adjustment mechanism will primarily focus on three core GATT obligations:

### A. Most-Favoured-Nation Treatment (GATT Article I:1)
Article I:1 requires that any advantage granted to products originating in one country must be accorded immediately and unconditionally to like products from all other Members. 

Under CBAM, exemptions or credit deductions are available if an exporting country has an explicit carbon price equivalent to the EU Emissions Trading System (EU ETS). Claimants argue that conditioning border treatment on the domestic environmental policies of exporting states creates de facto discrimination between "like" goods originating from different trading partners.

### B. National Treatment (GATT Article III:2 and III:4)
Under Article III, imported products must not be subject to internal taxes or regulatory burdens greater than those applied to like domestic products. 

A central point of contention is whether embedded emissions constitute a valid basis to differentiate products. Under long-standing GATT/WTO jurisprudence (*US – Malt Beverages*, *EC – Asbestos*), products sharing identical physical characteristics and end-uses are typically treated as "like," regardless of **Non-Product-Related Processes and Production Methods (npr-PPMs)**. If low-carbon steel and high-carbon steel are legally "like," imposing differential fiscal burdens at the border runs afoul of Article III:2.

### C. Tariff Bindings (GATT Article II:1)
If a panel determines that CBAM certificates do not constitute an internal measure under Article III, the requirement to surrender certificates may be characterized as an ordinary customs duty or an "other duty or charge" exceeding the importing state’s bound tariff commitments under Article II:1(b).

---

## 2. The Defense: Justification Under GATT Article XX

If a prima facie breach of Articles I, II, or III is established, the defending jurisdiction must rely on the general exceptions contained in GATT Article XX.
